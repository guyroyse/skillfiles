# Social Posts

This skill covers the shape of a Guy social post. The voice — register, rhythm,
word choice, and how much flourish to allow — comes from
**write-like-guy:voice**. Load it first; this skill assumes it.

## Platforms

Guy posts mainly on **X** and **LinkedIn**. Write for X unless he says
otherwise, and offer a LinkedIn version when the post would fit there too.

- **X** is the more casual of the two, and the edgier. Off-color jokes, sharper
  takes, and more of a bar-room tone are all fair game. Posts can run long when
  they need to, but shorter usually lands better. @mentions fit.
- **LinkedIn** is still fun and can still be edgy, just less so. Dial the edge
  back a notch rather than turning it into corporate voice. It runs a touch
  longer.

Guy rarely uses hashtags on either platform. Leave them off unless he asks.

When writing both versions of one post, keep the substance the same and change
only the tone and the platform details.

## Short, witty posts

Guy also posts short, witty one-liners on X, and he mostly writes those himself.
Don't offer one unprompted. If he asks for one, give him a few options to pick
from rather than one finished post.

These are real ones:

> The cyberattacks continue. I went to McDonalds and the ice cream machine was
> broken.

> The last several weeks have reinforced my belief that my appliances, my cars,
> and my house should not be on the Internet. Low tech is go tech. High tech is
> die tech.

> Totally not a cyberattack. Totally.

> Erudite groupthink is the best groupthink.

What they have in common:

- **They riff on what's in the news.** Most of them are a wry take on whatever
  tech story everyone is talking about that week, and they assume the reader
  already knows it. Don't explain the story.
- **Deadpan.** The setup is played straight, in a serious or newsy tone, and the
  joke is in the drop: a grave "the cyberattacks continue" lands on a broken ice
  cream machine. That's the voice skill's pattern-break, at its smallest.
- **One move per post.** A rhyming slogan ("Low tech is go tech. High tech is
  die tech."), a repeated word that says the opposite ("Totally. Totally."), or
  a register clash ("Erudite groupthink"). Never more than one.
- **Nothing else.** One to three sentences. No links, no hashtags, no @mentions,
  no emoji.

## The shape of a promo post

A typical Guy promo post moves like this (adapt freely — it's a groove, not a
form):

1. **Hook** — a plain, slightly disarming opener. Often a confession of
   obscurity or a "you've probably never heard of this."
2. **What it is** — the tl;dr. One sentence, compressed, technically honest.
3. **The human bit** — why he's posting. "I had the pleasure of hanging out with
   @so-and-so to nerd out about it." Names real people with @handles.
4. **The thing** — the link to the video, repo, or post, given room to breathe
   on its own line, not buried mid-sentence.
5. **The close** — a casual endorsement, the way a friend would recommend
   something ("It's a rabbit hole worth going down."), plus a soft invitation
   ("try it and leave feedback," "drop by and say hi").

Keep it short. One flourish. Land the ending — drafts most often fail by
trailing off, so the close should feel like a close.

## Worked examples

These are real Guy posts. Study the texture, don't clone the words.

**Promoting a project:**

> I like decentralized networks. In particular, I'm a fan of Reticulum.
> Reticulum is a fully-encrypted, anonymous, and decentralized mesh network that
> runs over all sorts of hardware—from Ethernet to LoRa radios to serial cables.
>
> It has a really cool page hosting thing called Nomad Network. It's
> delightfully text-based and reminds me of my BBS days back in the 90s.
>
> However—while I love the retro nature of this—it's not ideal for a lot of
> users. So, I wrote RServer and MeshBrowser. These are a web server and a web
> browser respectively that work over Reticulum. The code is very alpha but it,
> as they say, works on my machine.
>
> If you're interested, check out the links below. Try it out and leave some
> feedback so I can improve it all.

The plain "I like…" opener, the personal BBS aside, "very alpha" and "works on
my machine" as the seam showing, and a feedback invite to close. No hype words
anywhere.

**Promoting an appearance:**

> You probably haven't heard of the Reticulum Network. The tl;dr—it's an
> alternative network stack that's decentralized, encrypted, and runs on
> everything from Ethernet to LoRa radios to serial cables.
>
> I had the pleasure of hanging out with @emcommsolutions and @defidude
> yesterday to nerd out about it. It's a fun conversation and the video is up on
> Jake's channel. Give it a gander.
>
> [link on its own line]
>
> If you want to learn more or get started yourself, everything you need is over
> at reticulum.network. It's a rabbit hole worth going down.

The tl;dr move, "nerd out," real @handles, the link on its own line, and a
fragment ("Give it a gander.") before a close that actually ends.
